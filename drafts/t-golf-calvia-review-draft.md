# T Golf Calvià Review

I teed off at 15:20 on a Tuesday afternoon and the course was quiet enough that I could hear the wind moving through the pine trees between shots. From most of the fairways you cannot see a road or a building. The Tramuntana mountains sit in the background. For a course in the southwest corner of the island, that is not what you expect.

T Golf Calvià has 15 lakes and the layout uses them consistently. Several holes require carries from the tee and the water is in play on others through the approach. The course runs to just under 6,500 metres from the back tees where I played, with windmills dotted throughout the layout. Not an ornamental touch — they have been part of the course for years and they give it a character that is genuinely its own.

## The Conditioning

The fairways and fringe areas are very tightly mown. The bunkers are raked well and with a simple rake design that means the ball comes to rest against the rake much less often than you will find at most courses. It is a small detail and a really appreciated one if you have spent a round scrambling from awkward lies against a rake face.

The greens are large and pure. On the day I played there were no excuses for missing putts. Several holes also ask you to trust your distance completely because you cannot see the bottom of the flag from the approach. Players who rely on pin position to judge distance rather than working from yardage will be caught out.

## Some Holes Worth Knowing

Hole 7 is a dogleg through the trees. Short on the card but the approach distance is harder to read than it looks and players tend to underclub.

Hole 8 is a downhill par 4 with a tight landing area off the tee. The approach is a manageable wedge if you find the fairway, but miss it and a front pin gets difficult quickly. The slope makes it hard to stop the ball and running through is a real possibility.

Hole 10 is the clearest decision on the course. Dogleg right with a windmill on the left and water on the right. You can cut off distance over the water depending on how much you want to take on. It is the kind of hole that plays differently depending on where you are in the round.

Hole 16 is a par 4 uphill tee shot framed by rocks and trees. Visually one of the most distinct holes on the course and it plays harder than the card suggests.

Hole 18 is a tight par 5 that opens up as you move down the fairway. I hit driver, 6 iron, and just missed a 15-foot eagle putt. Finished with birdie. It is a proper finishing hole. It asks you to commit off what looks like a narrow tee shot, then gives you a real birdie or eagle chance if you do.

## Practical Information

**Green fees:** Up to €210 at peak times, from around €150 at twilight. T Golf Calvià runs midweek and multiple-visit offers regularly, worth checking before you book at [t-golf.club/calvia/offers](https://t-golf.club/calvia/offers/).

**Service:** Tees and water provided, friendly caddy master staff. The clubhouse and outdoor seating areas are well done. It is a good place to spend time before or after the round.

**Driving range:** Grass, which is not guaranteed at Mallorca clubs.

**One thing to note:** Signage around restricted buggy areas could be clearer. A couple of times I ended up somewhere that was not obviously marked as off limits. Not a problem once you know the course.

## Verdict

9/10. T Golf Calvià is one of the best-conditioned courses I have played in Majorca. The greens are excellent, the fairways are in great shape, and the layout tests you properly, particularly around distance judgement and water, without being unfair.

It suits players who want a serious round in good condition. The quality of the greens rewards players who can genuinely play. I would not put a high-handicapper here as their first course on a holiday trip, but for anyone on a dedicated golf visit it belongs on the itinerary. The twilight rate and the midweek offers make it good value at the right time.
