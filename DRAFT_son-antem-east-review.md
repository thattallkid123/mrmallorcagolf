Three or four groups were already queuing on the first tee when we arrived at 7:50 on a Sunday morning. We had booked early specifically to avoid the pace-of-play issues that resort courses can suffer from. We had not avoided them.

That tells you something useful about Son Antem East before you even hit a shot. It is a popular resort course in a well-run complex close to Palma, and it operates accordingly. If you go in with that expectation, you will enjoy it. If you expect a quiet, strategically demanding round, you may be disappointed.

### First Impression

Driving into the resort, the scale of it is immediately clear. Residences, extensive green space, a golf academy, paddle tennis courts, a hotel — it reads as a complete golf destination rather than a standalone course. The check-in process was organised, the staff were helpful, and a small shop near the entrance made it easy to pick up a coffee and a snack before the round. A practical detail, but a welcome one at 7:45 in the morning.

The setting itself is worth noting. The course sits in Llucmajor, 15–20 minutes from Palma. Out on the course — particularly on the back nine — you are surrounded by open countryside, the Randa mountains visible in the distance, no houses in sight, and enough wildlife around the fairways and water to make you look up from your scorecard occasionally. Rabbits, herons, and various birds are regular companions on the round.

### The Course

Son Antem East was designed by Francisco López Segalés and opened in 1995. It plays to a par 72 over around 6,890 yards from the back tees. The layout is generally open, the fairways generous, and tee shots on most holes carry little penalty for being slightly offline. That makes it accessible for mixed-ability groups and holiday golfers, and clearly that is what it was designed for.

The holes that stand out are the ones with more structure. The tree-lined holes are the most interesting from a playing perspective — they give you clearer visual lines and ask you to commit to a shape and a starting point off the tee. The more open sections, by contrast, are pleasant enough but do not demand much in the way of decision-making.

A few holes are worth mentioning specifically. Hole 10 is a dogleg-right with water short of the green, which creates a proper decision on the approach. Hole 12 is a relatively straightforward par 4, but the Randa mountain backdrop makes it one of the nicest-looking holes on the back nine. Hole 16 is an uphill dogleg-right par 5 through the trees, with a small, protected green — one of the better holes on the property.

The greens are small and undulating. They roll very purely — noticeably so — though the speed sits in the middle of the range rather than being especially quick. Around the greens, several are raised or slope away from the player. Worth knowing before you reach for a lob wedge: the ball releases considerably more after landing than it first appears it will, and a lower running shot is often the better play.

Water features appear on several holes and add some visual interest to certain tee shots. The 18th runs water along the full left side — straightforward to avoid if you are hitting it reasonably, but present enough to concentrate the mind.

### Conditioning

For a course that was handling a full book of tee times on a Sunday morning, the conditioning was very good. Fairways were solid, the greens rolled purely, and the overall presentation held up well under the traffic.

There was very little wind during the round, which meant conditions were as benign as they get. From the back tees the course did not feel especially long even in calm air.

### Walkability

Walking is completely fine. The routing is straightforward and the terrain is not demanding. There were a couple of moments where the path between holes was not entirely obvious on foot, but nothing that affected the round meaningfully. For anyone who prefers to walk rather than take a buggy, this is an easy course to do that on.

### Honest Negative

Many holes on the East course are strategically forgettable. The open layout means tee shots carry little consequence across a significant portion of the round, and the approach shots to those holes often feel routine. Compared to Son Gual, Alcanada, or Andratx — courses in a similar or slightly higher price range — Son Antem East asks fewer questions and leaves fewer memories. At €105, that gap is worth considering before booking.

### Verdict

7/10. Son Antem East is a well-run resort course with good conditioning, reliable service, and a layout that keeps most players comfortable from start to finish. The tree-lined holes and the water holes are the best of it. A significant portion of the course lacks strategic depth, and at €105 it competes with courses that ask more of you. It suits holiday golfers, mixed-ability groups, and anyone looking for an enjoyable and accessible round in a convenient setting close to Palma. It is not the right choice for a golfer specifically looking for a challenge.
