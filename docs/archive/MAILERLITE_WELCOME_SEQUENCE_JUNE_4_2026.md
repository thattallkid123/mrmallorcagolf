Most groups start with the obvious list:

- Son Gual
- Alcanada
- Son Muntaner

What kind of golf week are you actually trying to have?

- If you want one standout day, Son Gual often belongs in the conversation.

That is the point of the selector. It should narrow the field before you start booking tee times.

## Email 2

- Send: 2 days later
- Subject: `The mistake most golfers make planning Mallorca`
- Preview text: `It is usually not the course choice on its own.`

Body:

Hi,

The mistake I see most often is simple:

People try to book the "best" courses without thinking enough about order, travel, cost, and who is in the group.

A few examples:

- Two expensive headline courses back to back can make the third day feel flat.
- A course that is excellent for low handicaps can be a poor fit for a mixed group holiday.
- A good-value course at the right point in the trip can improve the whole week more than another premium round.
- Some golfers spend too much on green fees, then realise too late they also need buggy costs, club hire, taxis, or hotel transfers.

Mallorca is not difficult to plan, but it is easy to plan badly.

What usually works better is:

1. Pick the one or two rounds that really matter.
2. Build the rest of the week around pace, travel, and budget.
3. Match the courses to the group rather than chasing reputation alone.

That is where local context helps.

Next email, I will show you how I would think about course choice for different types of groups.

Andy

## Email 3

- Send: 5 days after signup
- Subject: `How to choose courses for your group`
- Preview text: `The right answer changes quickly once you know who is actually coming.`

Body:

Hi,

When someone asks me, "What are the best courses in Mallorca?", the honest answer is always:

Best for who?

A few examples:

If you have a strong golfing group:

- you may want one proper championship test
- green fee may matter less
- pace of play and course condition matter more

If you have a mixed group:

- the layout matters as much as prestige
- a punishing course can make the day feel long very quickly
- good practice areas, buggy use, and atmosphere can matter more than ranking lists

If this is a couple's or small private trip:

- travel time
- lunch
- overall setting
- whether the day feels relaxed

all start to matter more.

This is why one shortlist does not fit everyone.

A sensible Mallorca trip usually comes from balancing:

- one or two "must play" rounds
- one value round
- one easier or more relaxed day if the group needs it

If you want to skip the trial and error, this is exactly the kind of planning I help with.

You can see that here:

`https://www.mrmallorcagolf.com/plan-your-trip`

Andy
